## About This Programme

Waste is one of the most visible environmental challenges facing communities around the world. From plastic in our oceans to overflowing landfills, the effects of poor waste management touch every country we work in — yet they are rarely taught in schools in a meaningful, age-appropriate way.

Happy Green World's Waste & Recycling programme changes that. Through hands-on activity guides, student workbooks, and creative exercises, children learn where waste comes from, what happens to it, and — most importantly — what *they* can do about it.

## What Children Learn

- The difference between biodegradable and non-biodegradable materials
- How decomposition works, and why it matters
- The life cycle of everyday objects (from creation to disposal)
- How plastic enters rivers, oceans, and food chains
- Practical strategies for reducing waste at home and at school
- The value of gifting, sharing, and lending instead of buying new

## Activity Modules

Our Waste programme includes the following activity modules, each designed to be delivered in a single 45–60 minute session:

- **Decomposing Waste** (All ages, 45 mins) — students explore natural decomposition processes through observation and experiment
- **Reactions of Minerals to Soil, Water and Air** (All ages, 45 mins) — teaches the difference between biodegradable and non-biodegradable materials
- **Gifting, Sharing and Lending** (Ages 4–10, 45 mins) — focuses on waste reduction through the joy of reuse and sharing
- **Plastic Soup** (Ages 10–16, 45 mins) — explores the reality and science of ocean plastic pollution
- **Reduce Food Waste** (Ages 7–16, 60 mins) — includes practical cooking demonstrations to show how food waste can be prevented
- **The Effects of Waste** (All ages, 60 mins) — broad environmental impact education connecting waste to wider ecosystems
- **The Life of…** (All ages, 60 mins) — a creative writing exercise encouraging children to imagine the journey of a waste material

## Localisation

Every module is adapted to reflect the specific waste challenges facing the community it is taught in. A classroom in Ghana learns about different types of waste than one in the Netherlands — and our illustrations and examples reflect that.
